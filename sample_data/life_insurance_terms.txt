Life Insurance Business Terms and Definitions

This document contains key business terms used in life insurance operations.

POLICY TERMS:

Sum Assured: The guaranteed amount that will be paid to beneficiaries upon the death of the insured or at policy maturity. Also known as death benefit or face amount. Example: A policy with sum assured of $500,000 means beneficiaries receive $500,000 upon the insured's death.

Premium: The periodic payment made by the policyholder to keep the insurance policy active. Can be paid monthly, quarterly, or annually. Example: Monthly premium of $150 for a term life policy.

Policy Term: The duration for which the insurance coverage is valid. Example: 20-year term policy, whole life policy (lifetime coverage).

Maturity Date: The date when the policy term ends and benefits become payable if the insured is still alive (for endowment policies). Example: Policy issued on Jan 1, 2020 with 20-year term matures on Jan 1, 2040.

Surrender Value: The amount the policyholder receives if they terminate the policy before maturity. Calculated as total premiums paid minus surrender charges and policy administration fees. Formula: Surrender Value = Total Premiums Paid - Surrender Charges - Admin Fees - Cost of Insurance.

Paid-Up Value: The reduced sum assured that continues without further premium payments after a policy has acquired surrender value. Example: Policy with $100,000 sum assured may have paid-up value of $45,000 after 5 years.

UNDERWRITING TERMS:

Sum at Risk: The difference between sum assured and the policy reserve at any given time. This represents the insurer's net exposure. Formula: Sum at Risk = Sum Assured - Policy Reserve.

Mortality Rate: The probability of death within a specific age group in a given year. Used for premium calculation and reserve estimation. Example: Mortality rate for males aged 40-45 might be 0.002 or 0.2%.

Medical Underwriting: The process of assessing the health and lifestyle of applicants to determine insurability and premium rates. Results in classification: Standard, Sub-standard, or Declined.

Free Look Period: The timeframe (typically 15-30 days) during which a policyholder can cancel a new policy and receive a full refund of premiums paid.

CLAIM TERMS:

Death Claim: Payment made to beneficiaries upon proof of the insured's death. Amount equals the sum assured plus any bonuses minus outstanding loans.

Maturity Claim: Payment made when an endowment policy reaches its maturity date and the insured is alive. Amount equals sum assured plus accumulated bonuses.

Claim Settlement Ratio: Percentage of claims settled versus claims received. Formula: (Number of Claims Settled / Total Claims Received) * 100. Example: 95% settlement ratio means 95 out of 100 claims were approved and paid.

FINANCIAL METRICS:

Policy Reserve: The amount an insurer must maintain to meet future policy obligations. Calculated using actuarial methods based on mortality tables and interest assumptions.

New Business Premium: Total annualized premium from new policies sold in a period. Key growth metric for life insurance companies.

Persistency Ratio: Percentage of policies that remain in force (active). Formula: (Number of Active Policies at Year End / Number of Policies at Year Start) * 100. Example: 88% persistency means 88 out of 100 policies remained active.

Expense Ratio: Total expenses as a percentage of total premium income. Formula: (Total Operating Expenses / Total Premium Income) * 100. Used to measure operational efficiency.

BENEFICIARY TERMS:

Nominee: Person designated to receive policy benefits in case of the insured's death. Can be changed during the policy term.

Assignee: Person to whom policy rights have been legally transferred. Common in loan scenarios where policy is assigned to lender as security.

Beneficiary: The person or entity entitled to receive insurance proceeds. Can be primary or contingent beneficiaries.
